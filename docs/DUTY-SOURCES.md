## Sources

1. **Classification.** Neither ruling states an HTS number: [N328954](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N328954) (15 Nov 2022, origin China) and [N357771](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N357771) (29 Jan 2026, origin Thailand) are origin-only. Subheading inferred from [HQ H317418](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/H317418) (13 May 2025): heat guns in 8419.89.9585. **Unverified for the Kobalt model:** a CBP rulings search for "Kobalt heat gun" returns only the two origin rulings, so no ruling classifies it; confirm with a broker. (HQ 952881, 1993, used 8516.32/8516.79; the 2025 ruling is later.)
2. **MFN 4.2%** (Other column 35%). [HTS 8419.89.95](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=8419.89.95), Rev. 21, 15 Oct 2026; H317418 also states 4.2%. Primary.
3. **Origin.** Both rulings apply substantial transformation to Section 301 and other additional duties; N357771 holds Thailand when motor, PCBA, fan and heating element are made in Thailand. Primary.
4. **Section 301 China = List 2, +25%.** [9903.88.02](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.88.02) = duty + 25%, with 9903.88.12 among its carve-outs. USTR's [2019 exclusion notice, 84 FR 37381](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/07/31/2019-16256/notice-of-product-exclusions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology-transfer) (31 Jul 2019) lists heat guns (8419.89.9585) under the $16B List 2 action, and H317418 cites 9903.88.12 for them. That exclusion is expired and not in the current 178-line set (9903.88.69, through 9 Nov 2026, [HTS](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.88.69)); I did not find heat guns in that set (not exhaustively checked). The List 2 FR notice itself (83 FR 40823, 16 Aug 2018) was not opened; 25% rate is from the HTS heading text. Earlier draft said List 3; corrected.
5. **Section 301 forced-labor (24 Jul 2026).** Primary: [91 FR 47318](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies) (FR Doc 2026-15181, 28 Jul 2026) imposes 12.5% on products of China (item 12) and of Thailand (item 53), "except as provided in Annex I and Annex II, Part A"; HTS headings 9903.05.31 (China) and 9903.05.77 (Thailand), duty + 12.5%. **Exemptions checked by OCR:** the Annex I/II lists are images, so I rendered all 345 pages of the PDF and OCR'd them (macOS Vision). 8419.89.95 / 8419.89 appears in none; only 8419.50, 8419.81, 8419.90 lines appear (Annex II Part A, PDF pp. 149-150). So the item is not exempt, to the extent OCR is reliable (spot-checked, not proofread). Stacking on China 301 is primary (re-checked 17 Oct): HTS Ch. 99 U.S. note 52(a), also in the notice at Annex I (FR p. 47352), says products subject to 9903.05.20–9903.05.84 "shall also be subject to any additional duty provided for in this subchapter or in subchapter IV of chapter 99", and 9903.88.02 is in subchapter III. A text-layer search of the Rev. 21 Ch. 99 PDF confirms 8419.89 is in none of note 52(b)–(k).
6. **IEEPA.** *Learning Resources v. Trump*, 20 Feb 2026, 6-3 ([CRS LSB11398](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11398)); the FR also carries [Ending Certain Tariff Actions](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03832/ending-certain-tariff-actions) (2026-03832, 25 Feb 2026). Secondary for the ruling; the termination document title is primary, its text was not read.
7. **Section 122.** Primary: [Proclamation, 91 FR 9339](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03824/imposing-a-temporary-import-surcharge-to-address-fundamental-international-payments-problems) imposes 10% for 150 days effective 24 Feb 2026; 150 days ends 24 Jul 2026, and the statute allows longer only by Act of Congress. The forced-labor tariffs start 12:01 a.m. 24 Jul 2026. I found no extension in the Federal Register; absence of an extension is not proven.
8. **Section 232.** Not covered. I OCR'd the annexes of [Proclamation 2026-06960](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/09/2026-06960/strengthening-actions-taken-to-adjust-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states) (9 Apr 2026, 66 pages): only 8419.81.50 and 8516 parts/appliance lines appear, not 8419.89.95; the Aug 2025 inclusions notice ([2025-15819](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/19/2025-15819/adoption-and-procedures-of-the-section-232-steel-and-aluminum-tariff-inclusions-process)) text likewise. OCR-based, not proofread. Effect on the gap would be nil anyway.
9. **Not checked:** anti-dumping, actions after 14 Oct 2026, merchandise processing/harbor fees (same for both).

## Caveats

- The ~25 point gap rests on the List 2 25% (HTS heading text plus the 2019 exclusion notice) and on no current exclusion applying; the heat-gun exclusion is expired.
- Forced-labor 301 is under legal challenge; the 9903.88.69 exclusions end 9 Nov 2026 unless extended.

- **Malaysia exemption not ruled out.** Malaysia has a country-specific list (U.S. note 52(j)(4)(i), heading 9903.05.99) plus Annex II Part E. 8419.89 is not in the note list or Annex II Part A (OCR, not proofread), but Part E was not checked. If it is exempt, Malaysia's total would be 4.2%.
- Section 232, Section 122 (expired) and IEEPA (invalidated) add nothing, as above. Anti-dumping was not checked.

### Sources

1. **Classification (inferred; no ruling on this model).** The Hesai rulings [N359371](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N359371) (25 Mar 2026; scenario I Thailand, scenario II with PCBAs made in Vietnam = Vietnam) and [N363700](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N363700) (7 Aug 2026) are origin-only. The closest classification rulings, [N282920](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N282920), [N282922](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N282922) and [N282926](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N282926) (2017, LeddarTech LiDAR modules for autonomous-vehicle collision avoidance), use **9015.80.2000** and cite [HQ H245890](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/H245890) for LiDAR in heading 9015. Competing line: 9015.10.40 (electrical rangefinders; [NY G82747](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/G82747), a laser-radar cruise sensor). No ruling found uses 8526.10 or 9031.80 for LiDAR.

Actions continued by [91 FR 64212](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/07/2026-20510/continuation-of-actions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology-transfer) (7 Oct 2026). ### Caveats

- **Classification is the main risk.** Under 9015.10.40 instead, China 301 would still be 25% (List 3), but if the module counts as a passenger-vehicle or light-truck part, Section 232 autos +25% (9903.94.05) would apply, which exempts it from forced-labor 301 (note 52(f)(3)): about China 50%, others 25%. Get a binding ruling.
- Forced-labor 301 is under legal challenge.
