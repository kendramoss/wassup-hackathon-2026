## Sources

1. **Classification.** Neither ruling states an HTS number: [N328954](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N328954) (15 Nov 2022, origin China) and [N357771](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/N357771) (29 Jan 2026, origin Thailand) are origin-only. Subheading inferred from [HQ H317418](https://rulings.cbp.gov/api/ruling/H317418) (13 May 2025): heat guns in 8419.89.9585. **Unverified for the Kobalt model:** no CBP ruling classifies it; confirm with a broker. (HQ 952881, 1993, used 8516.32/8516.79; the 2025 ruling is later.)
2. **MFN 4.2%** (Other column 35%). [HTS 8419.89.95](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=8419.89.95), Rev. 21, 15 Oct 2026; H317418 also states 4.2%. Primary.
3. **Origin.** Both rulings apply substantial transformation to Section 301 and other additional duties; N357771 holds Thailand when motor, PCBA, fan and heating element are made in Thailand. Primary.
4. **Section 301 China = List 2, +25%.** [9903.88.02](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.88.02) = duty + 25%, with 9903.88.12 among its carve-outs. USTR's [2019 exclusion notice, 84 FR 37381](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2019/07/31/2019-16256/notice-of-product-exclusions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology-transfer) (31 Jul 2019) lists heat guns (8419.89.9585) under the $16B List 2 action, and H317418 cites 9903.88.12 for them. That exclusion is expired and not in the current 178-line set (9903.88.69, through 9 Nov 2026, [HTS](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.88.69)); I did not find heat guns in that set (not exhaustively checked). The List 2 FR notice itself (83 FR 40823, 16 Aug 2018) was not opened; 25% rate is from the HTS heading text. Earlier draft said List 3; corrected.
5. **Section 301 forced-labor (new, 24 Jul 2026).** HTS headings [9903.05.31 (China)](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.05) and 9903.05.77 (Thailand), each duty + 12.5%, U.S. note 52; USTR notice 91 FR 47318 (28 Jul 2026, citation from secondary summaries). Exemption headings 9903.05.85-92 cover transit goods, notes 52(b)-(f) (civil aircraft, pharma, Section 232 goods, etc.). I did not read note 52(b)/(c) text, so whether this item is exempt is **unverified**; I found no sign it is. If exempt, both totals drop 12.5 points and the gap is unchanged. Stacking with China 301 per a [secondary guide](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/country-guides/united-states/section-301-forced-labor) (unverified).
6. **IEEPA.** *Learning Resources v. Trump*, 20 Feb 2026, 6-3, IEEPA does not authorize tariffs ([CRS LSB11398](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11398)). Secondary summary of the decision.
7. **Section 122.** 10% heading [9903.03.01](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/search?keyword=9903.03) exists (primary); expiry 24 Jul 2026 is from secondary sources ([Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3462)); unverified against a notice.
8. **Section 232.** Not on the steel/aluminum/copper derivative lists as far as I could find, but I did not open CBP's Annex I list (CSMS #65936570) to search 8419.89; **unverified**. Effect on the gap is nil: 232 duties would apply to both origins, and heading 9903.05.90 removes the forced-labor 12.5% on 232-covered goods.
9. **Not checked:** anti-dumping, actions after 14 Oct 2026, merchandise processing/harbor fees (same for both).

## Caveats

- The ~25 point gap rests on the List 2 25% (HTS heading text plus the 2019 exclusion notice) and on no current exclusion applying; the heat-gun exclusion is expired.
- Forced-labor 301 is under legal challenge; the 9903.88.69 exclusions end 9 Nov 2026 unless extended.
