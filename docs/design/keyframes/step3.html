Step 3 key frames: foundations

Six directions (A–C first, D–F from similar products on Mobbin below) for colour, type and spacing, each applied to the same locked screen: layout b, the heat-gun Result with the recorded endpoint response. Each comes with its token sheet. All three use one accent for "supported" and one warm colour for "look again"; every text colour passes WCAG AA on its page colour (contrast measured, shown on the sheets). Spacing is the same 4 px scale in all three; only corner radius differs. Checks: ✓ pass, ~ partial, ✗ fail.

A · Ruling letter Recommended

Warm paper and an editorial serif, like a CBP ruling letter. Source Serif 4, Inter, IBM Plex Mono. Green for supported, amber for look again. 8 px corners.

Principle 3, "document, not dashboard": reads as a letter, not an app
Serif verdict gives the 10-second headline the most weight
Stands out from the usual dashboard look in a field of hackathon entries
Two type families plus mono: keep the serif to verdict and headings only

B · Federal register

Public Sans is the US government's own typeface (US Web Design System), so the app looks like an official form. Cool white, navy ink, federal blue, amber. Roboto Mono. 4 px corners.

Strong story for the Q&A: built in the typeface CBP's own sites use
One family: the simplest to build and keep consistent
Looks official, which may read as "this is a ruling" against our "not a ruling" line
Least memorable on video: close to every government form

C · Spec sheet

An engineering drawing: white, graphite, hairlines, mono labels. Inter Tight, Inter, JetBrains Mono. Teal for supported, signal orange for look again. 2 px corners.

Fits the Manufacturing theme and the factory user
Sharpest for dense plan tables in step 3 of the flow
Mono labels everywhere slow down a first-time reader
Closest to a dashboard, against principle 3

From similar products on Mobbin

Searched Mobbin for AI research with cited sources, legal and contract review, tax and compliance, and shipping tools. Most compliance and tax products (Vanta, QuickBooks, Xero, Shopify) share one look: a white SaaS dashboard with a single brand colour. That goes against principle 3, so it isn't here. Three looks that differ from A–C are below, built the same way.

D · Research report

After Elicit's research report: the result is a white page lying on a grey desk, verdict and titles in a deep-teal serif. Newsreader, Inter, IBM Plex Mono. 6 px corners.

The page-on-a-desk literally says "document": the strongest fit with principle 3
Teal verdict reads as a finding, not a warning
The draft for the broker (step 4 of the flow) can reuse the same page
Colour on the verdict competes with the green "supported" accent; they share one hue

E · Lab notebook

After Cohere's playground: warm beige, mono uppercase labels and chips, coral for "look again", deep green for "supported". Space Grotesk, DM Sans, DM Mono. 4 px corners.

Most "AI lab" in character: signals a measured, evaluated system
Coral makes "look again" impossible to miss
Uppercase mono chips are harder to read at a glance
Coral reads as an error, which undersells "needs expert review" as a safety step (principle 2)

F · Dark reading room Chosen

After Claude, Gemini deep research and Adaline in dark mode: charcoal page, light serif verdict, mint for supported, amber for look again. Source Serif 4, Inter, IBM Plex Mono.

Strongest on the 2:45 video and a projector: high contrast, little glare
Highest contrast for accent and amber (9:1)
Same type as A: could ship as A's dark mode rather than a separate direction
Printing solved: the broker draft is a light page inside the dark app (below)
