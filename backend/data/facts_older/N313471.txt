In your letter dated July 24, 2020, you requested a country of origin ruling on behalf of your client, Midea American Corporation.The merchandise under consideration is identified as the “vacuum cleaner the NEU10 Series, BDLCE10 Series and HDUE1 Series”, which includes 7 related models. The series models are bagless, electric-powered, “stick” vacuum cleaners designed for household use. The main differences between the three series are the appearance of the foot cover, exhaust grill, big wheels and dust cup cover. The vacuum’s power is 720W and its dust cup capacity is 1.7L. The vacuum cleaners are packaged for sale to individual consumers to be used as upright vacuums. Each unit is sold in a single package together with its corresponding accessories. The models within the series vary slightly because they are sold into different markets with different color schemes, different accessories, or slightly different features. However, all of the models use the same platform, with only one or two parts that might vary between models in the series.You present a processing scenario for the three series models for the country of origin determination. Under the scenario, the products’ materials and components are sourced from China and Vietnam and the final processing occurs in Vietnam. The NEU10 Series, BDLCE10 Series and HDUE1 Series models are made from roughly over 60 components and sub-assemblies. Approximately 49% (by unit cost) of the listed components and sub-assemblies, such as handle and body assemblies, foot hose and base assemblies, foot covers, nozzle assembly, cyclone, dust cup assembly and cover, etc., including the instructional manual and packaging materials, are made in Vietnam. The balance of parts and assemblies, such as the switches, power lines, motor seal cover, foot wheels and foot belt, brush roll assembly, hose assembly, filter, screws, etc., are imported from China.The laborers involved in the production of the vacuum cleaners in Vietnam include electricians, product engineers, test engineers and workers who are specially trained to ensure the product’s quality and specifications. Three key sub-assemblies, i.e., the dust cup sub-assembly, body sub-assembly and foot sub-assembly are made in the Vietnamese factory. The final assembly is conducted in a main assembly line, in which the major sub-assemblies are combined with other components for the production of the finished vacuums. This process consists of approximately 23 distinct and complex steps, generally including (A) assembly/manufacture; (B) testing; and (C) packing. Each step is carried out by a different worker.
