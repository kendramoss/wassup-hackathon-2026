In your letter dated June 10, 2020, you requested a country of origin ruling on behalf of your client, Walgreens Company.There are two cables at issue with this request, each designed to connect, charge, and sync a variety of mobile devices to host units or charging stations. The first is identified as the USB 2.0 A to C Cable 6-foot. The cable consists of a 6-foot length of insulated cable with a standard, male USB-A connector at one end and a male USB-C connector at the other. The second is identified as the USB 2.0 A to C braided 6-foot. This cable is identical to the first with the only difference being that the outer jacket of the cable is of a braided design. In your request, you state that the manufacturing process is fully completed in Vietnam and is the same for both cables. The process begins with raw Vietnamese cable being annealed, drawn, twisted, and jacketed prior to being insulated. The ends are then prepared for the addition of the Chinese connectors. The connectors are soldered to the ends before the cables are tested and packaged for shipment to the United States. The “country of origin” is defined in
