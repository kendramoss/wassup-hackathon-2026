In your letter dated March 6, 2020, you requested a country of origin marking ruling on behalf of your client, FGX International, Inc.The merchandise at issue is described as non-prescription sunglasses with frames made of either plastic or metal. In your request, you describe multiple manufacturing scenarios for the subject sunglasses that are detailed in the following paragraphs. The first scenario begins with the importation of empty frames of Chinese origin into the Philippines. Non-prescription, tinted lenses, also of Chinese origin, are cut and beveled before being placed within the frames to create finished sunglasses. The completed articles are then inspected, cleaned, tagged, and packaged for shipment to their final destination. The second scenario begins with the importation of empty frames of Chinese origin into Vietnam. Non-prescription, tinted lenses, also of Chinese origin, are cut and beveled before being placed within the frames to create finished sunglasses. The completed articles are then inspected, cleaned, tagged, and packaged for shipment to their final destination. In the third scenario, unassembled frames of Chinese origin, consisting of fronts and temples, are imported into the Philippines where they are assembled into empty frames. Non-prescription, tinted lenses, also of Chinese origin, are cut and beveled before being placed within the frames to create finished sunglasses. The completed articles are then inspected, cleaned, tagged, and packaged for shipment to their final destination. The fourth and final scenario begins with the importation of unassembled frames of Chinese origin, consisting of fronts and temples, into Vietnam where they are assembled into empty frames. Non-prescription, tinted lenses, also of Chinese origin, are cut and beveled before being placed within the frames to create finished sunglasses. The completed articles are then inspected, cleaned, tagged, and packaged for shipment to their final destination.
