In your letter dated January 2, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on five pairs of pliers, which are identified as follows: bent nose pliers, end cutting pliers, flat nose pliers, needle nose pliers, and hose grip pliers. Pictures of the tools in different stages of manufacture were included with your submission, along with a narrative of the manufacturing process. All five pliers have tapered jaws that are used for gripping, twisting, bending, cutting stripping and crimping wires and other small objects. They are made of two pieces of steel, with each piece providing half of the handles and half of the pair of jaws. When joined together, the joint provides a fulcrum that transfers and increases the force applied to the handles by the user to the jaws. The manufacturing process for the five pairs of pliers is substantially the same. It starts with drop-forging raw steel rods in Thailand into the shape and form of the two halves of the pliers (i.e., the pieces incorporating the handles and jaws). They are further die-cut and deburred to remove sharp edges and to form the contours and profiles of each tool. The articles are then sent to China, where they are drilled with holes and are joined together using rivet pins. The jaws are machined with teeth and/or cutting edges. The pliers are sandblasted, heat treated, polished, and soft plastic or rubber grips are applied to the handles. Finally, the pliers are adjusted for alignment, oiled and packaged for retail sale.
