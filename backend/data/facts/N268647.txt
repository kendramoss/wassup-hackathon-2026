In your letter dated September 11, 2015, you requested a ruling on the tariff classification and status of television-type cameras from Canada under the NAFTA.The merchandise under consideration is the Allied Vision Prosilica GC Series Cameras (in color or monochrome) with Gigabit Ethernet interface (GigE Vision™). These machine-vision cameras, which do not contain any inbuilt recording capability, capture images in real time and transmit them to a location outside the cameras for remote viewing and/or recording. These cameras are intended for use in various applications, such as in industrial inspection, surveillance, and robotics.It is noted that most of the main components of these cameras, such as nearly all of the PCB assemblies, originate in Canada and the U.S., with a few originating in Germany; and other components such as the housing, the back plate, and various types of rings originating in Canada. Moreover, there are components such as different types of hardware which originate from Taiwan, Canada, and the U.S., as well as sensors (depending upon the model) from Japan. All of the cameras’ components of non-Canadian origin are imported into Canada in different shipments and at different times, as well as in different quantities, depending upon production needs. The manufacturing steps that occur in Canada consist of the following: preparing the components such as the PCBA’s and the housing for assembling, as well as integrating the components by soldering the GC power PCBA to the GC back plate; attaching the GC interface PCBA and the GC control board into the GC Power PCBA; programming the firmware; modifying the analog PCBA; preparing the sensors; performing various tests (Trigger test, SYNC functionality test, and Current test); screwing the boards together; preparing the IR filter (for color CCD); placing the assembled boards into the housing; setting up the overnight burn-in; performing the CCD alignment test; ensuring image focus and acceptance; inspecting for irregularities; and cleaning and bagging.
