In your letter dated September 15, 2025, you requested a country of origin ruling on air conditioning filters. Descriptive literature was provided for our review. The items under consideration are described as automotive air filters, which are components of the automotive heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) system designed to clean the air entering the vehicle’s interior. The devices filter out dust, pollen, mold, bacteria, and other airborne particles, improving the air quality inside the car. The filter is usually located behind the glove compartment or under the dashboard. The WT333027 and WT342016 models are rectangular versions of the filter with a frame holding the filtering material in place. The automotive air conditioning filters are assembled in Thailand using components from Thailand and China. The Chinese components include the frames, hot melt adhesive, color box, and label. The Thai components include the functional element, origami, filter media, blister film, manual and other packaging materials. The assembly process occurs entirely in Thailand and includes seventeen steps. The process starts with the slitting of the non-woven fabric raw material using a slitting machine, followed by a media pleating machine fold line pressing and media pleating the fabric. Next, the functional element is shaped using a heat curing machine. Using a slitting knife, a worker then manually cuts the media from a three-dimensional filter block into small filter blocks of equal volume. The media is then processed for self-sealing using an ultrasonic welding machine. Next, a bevel cutting machine cuts the element before the filter media is brought to an adhesive injection machine to apply the back gum. The filter forming process continues with flash trimming, gasket bonding, and spraying data code. Finally, the filter is wrapped in plastic using a laminating machine and sent along a conveying line for placement and packing in a cardboard box.
