In your letter dated August 12, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on behalf of your client, SEI Optifrontier Vietnam, Ltd. The merchandise under consideration is described as optical fiber cables, identified by their individual model numbers AN003870 (Model 1), AN003871 (Model 2), and AN003920 (Model 3). Models 1 and 2 are further described as in-rack fan-out (IRFO) cables, which allow a multi-fiber cable to be split at the optical fiber endpoints into individual connections. Model 3 is further described as a multi-drop (MD) cable, which is a single cable with optical fibers that are branched out along a cable route to serve multiple locations or devices along its path. All three models are for use within various telecommunications industries to transfer optical data signals. In your request, you state that the manufacturing process for all three optical fiber cable models begins in Japan with the production of the glass preform. This preform is heated and drawn into thin optical fiber strands with precise diameter and transmission characteristics. Protective and reinforcing materials, such as aramid yarn and flame-retardant PVC, are then applied to the fiber. Prior to export to Vietnam, Model 3 undergoes additional processing in Japan. Multiple cords are stranded—twisted together and secured with plastic tape—to create a bundled cable configuration. This is followed by a jacketing process, where an additional outer protective PVC layer is extruded over the stranded cords. This creates the integrated cable structure required for the MD configuration, providing enhanced mechanical protection and environmental resistance. In Vietnam, the cables are cut to the required lengths. The fiber ends are then processed, grouped in the required order, stripped, and inserted into the ferrule using adhesive. The ferrule and fiber then undergo polishing and surface inspection to ensure compliance with shape specifications and visual standards. Finally, connectors are assembled and secured to the cord via crimping and heating at both ends. The finished product is then bagged and packed into cartons or reels for export to the United States.
