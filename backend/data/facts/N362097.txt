In your letter dated June 5, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on behalf of your client, GB II, Inc. There are three products under consideration. The first is the Minimalist® Drop Point Fixed Blade Knife, model 2384K. It is a compact fixed blade outdoor survival knife. The knife features a high carbon stainless steel blade of just over two inches with a black stonewash finish and a scaled G10 handle with contoured finger grooves. It comes with a thermoplastic sheath with mounting options to allow the knife to be carried on a belt, attached to gear, or worn around the neck. The second is the Drifter Liner Lock Manual Folding Knife, model 6450K. It is an everyday carry folding pocket knife with a manually opened, locking blade. The knife features a high carbon stainless steel blade of nearly three inches with a titanium nitride finish and a scaled G10 handle. Once opened, the blade is held in place by an internal linerlock that can be disengaged with one hand to close the knife. It includes a pocket clip to allow the knife to be carried in a pocket when not in use. The third is the Ignitor® Assisted Opening Knife, model 6885. It is a folding pocket knife with an assisted-opening, locking blade. The knife has a 3.5-inch high carbon stainless steel drop point blade that is coated for corrosion resistance and a black scaled G10 handle. The blade features oversized, scalloped serrations along a portion of the cutting edge which are intended to improve performance when cutting fibrous materials such as rope, straps, or similar line. The blade deployment is manually initiated by the user pulling on the flipper tab and then deployed to the fully open position by the assisted opening mechanism. We note that your correspondence indicates this knife uses the same “OutBurst” assisted-opening mechanism addressed in Headquarters ruling W116730, dated November 7, 2006, which determined those knives were not considered switchblades and would be permitted unrestricted entry into the United States. Once opened, the blade is held in place by an internal linerlock that can be disengaged with one hand to close the knife. It is equipped with a pocket clip that permits carriage inside a pocket when not in use. 

The production process involves two countries. In Vietnam, stainless steel sheet from China is transformed into dimensionally final, model-specific knife blade blanks through laser cutting, surface grinding, and flattening to the knife’s final thickness. These blanks are then sent to China where they undergo deburring, punch removal of the handling loops, heat treatment, sharpening, surface finishing, and mechanical assembly with the handle components.
