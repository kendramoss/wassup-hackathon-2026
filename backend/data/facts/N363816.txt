In your letter dated July 29, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on throttle bodies. The products at issue are throttle bodies, specifically item numbers E57C and E87-1. These components are part of a motor vehicle’s engine air-intake system. They are designed to execute commands received from the engine’s Electronic Control Unit (ECU) by opening and closing the throttle plate to control the volume of air intake. Concurrently, they provide output signals that allow the control system to monitor the working status of the throttle body. Both throttle bodies are assemblies primarily comprised of a valve body, a cover, an actuating motor, a position sensor, and a transition gear on a gear shaft. With respect to origin, the throttle body is assembled in Thailand using a valve body from Thailand, a software chip from Germany and other components primarily sourced from China. The final assembly begins by manually installing a shaft seal onto the valve body. Next, one to two bearings and a transition gear shaft are press-fit into position, followed by the installation of a torsion spring. The throttle shaft assembly is then loaded, twisted, and pressed into position. Subsequently, a shaft collar is punched onto the base of the valve body. The throttle plate, socket assembly, motor assembly, German-manufactured chip, and Chinese-manufactured sensor assembly are then installed in sequence. The process finishes with the tightening of screws, the soldering of motor terminals, and final programming. For item number E57C, additional steps occur. Such steps include the manual application of lubricating oil on the seal ring, followed by the installation of a side cover and a crimp plug. Testing and inspections are conducted throughout the assembly process.
