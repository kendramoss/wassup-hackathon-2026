In your letter dated April 18, 2022, on behalf of your client, Samuel, Son & Co. (USA) Inc., you are requesting a tariff classification and a country of origin ruling for aluminum extrusions. Also, you inquire whether the aluminum extrusions are eligible for duty free treatment under the United States-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) Trade Agreement and if Section 232 additional duties are applicable.The products under consideration are aluminum extrusions/profiles for use as frames for door frames, window frames and solar panels. According to your submission, the extrusions/profiles are made from aluminum alloy types 6063, 6061 and 6005. The extrusions will be imported to the United States (U.S.) in two different scenarios.In the first scenario, aluminum alloy billets are manufactured in the U.S. You state that the billets are produced by melting ingots sourced from Canada, Australia, India, etc. which are mixed with U.S. aluminum scrap and then cast into aluminum alloy logs. None of the materials used to make the billets originate from China or Russia. The billets range from 6 to 9 inches in diameter and 20 to 228 inches in length. Next, the billets are exported to Mexico where they are initially cut/trimmed, heated, extruded, cut to the requested length per customer’s specifications, anodized, painted, inspected, packaged and then shipped to the U.S. You indicate that some of the extrusions will be hollow (i.e., having one enclosed void), and others will be of an open construction. In the second scenario, aluminum alloy billets of South Korean origin or other source countries (not China or Russia) are imported into Mexico. The billets are manufactured in the same manner as the first scenario and range from 6 to 9 inches in diameter and 20 to 228 inches in length. In Mexico, the billets are first cut/trimmed, heated, extruded, cut to the requested length per customer’s specifications, anodized, painted, inspected, packaged and then shipped to the U.S. You state that some of the profiles will be hollow, and others will not (i.e., open construction).
