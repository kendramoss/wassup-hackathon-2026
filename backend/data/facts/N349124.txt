In your letter dated May 17, 2025, you requested a country of origin ruling on Valentine’s Marshmallow Pops. A manufacturing flow chart, list of product ingredients, spec. sheets, photos, and product samples accompanied your request. The product samples were disposed of. The subject merchandise is described as Valentine’s Marshmallow Pop composed of sugar (product of Thailand), corn syrup (product of China), water (product of Cambodia), dextrose, gelatin, corn starch, artificial flavor (products of China), Arabic gum (product of France), maltodextrin (product of China), FD&C Red No. 40, Beet Powder, FD&C Yellow No. 5, FD&C Yellow No. 6, FD&C Blue No. 1 (products of India). You have stated that all manufacturing is conducted in Cambodia, in which raw ingredients of sugar, corn syrup, dextrose, corn starch, and water are boiled. Artificial flavoring and FD&C Red No. 40, FD&C Blue No. 1, FD&C Yellow No. 5 and No. 6 are added and blended. The product is then molded and cooled. The resulting products are shaped like a giftbox, a rainbow, and a pink car. Each item is placed atop a lollipop stick and the Marshmallow Pops are individually wrapped in transparent plastic. The giftbox, rainbow, and pink car are packaged in cardboard boxes with a net weight of 116 grams. The merchandise is destined for the candy market.
