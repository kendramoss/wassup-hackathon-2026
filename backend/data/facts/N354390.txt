In your letter dated September 29, 2025, you requested a country of origin ruling for marking purposes under Section 304 of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended (19 U.S.C. 1304). Pictures and descriptions of the manufacturing processes were included in your submission. The item under consideration is a tamper. It is a hand tool used for tamping down asphalt, gravel, stone, and soil. The tool is made of steel and consists of either a 10" x 10" or 8" x 8" base, with four reinforcement ribs attached to the top portion of the base, and a steel handle with a plastic grip applied to its end. The manufacturing process for the tamper begins in Vietnam, where raw steel is laser-cut into the base of the tool and the four reinforcement ribs. The base is then embossed with a steel die, and it is welded together with the four reinforcement ribs. The fully formed tamper head is subsequently sent to China, where it is welded to a metal handle and the finished assembly is sandblasted and powder coated. Finally, a plastic grip is applied to the end of the handle and the finished tamper is then shipped to the United States.
