The glasses at issue are artificial intelligence (“AI”) smart glasses that offer users the opportunity to capture photos and videos, make video calls or livestream, store photos and videos, play music, make phone calls, send texts using voice commands, and talk with AI. The Smart Glasses have a front frame, right and left temple arms, and two lenses. The lenses of the Smart Glasses will be available as prescription or non-prescription, depending on what the customer chooses. The customer also has the option to select from clear or transition lenses. The Smart Glasses are within the same product family as the smart glasses covered by Headquarters Ruling Letter (“HQ”) H345669, dated July 23, 2025; HQ H348154, dated August 26, 2025; and HQ H348983, dated October 20, 2025. The instant glasses will include the following enhancements: slimmer temple arms; adjustable nose pads that offer a custom fit, boost comfort, and prevent slipping; removed camera cover window; overextension hinge for user comfort; three different styles and multiple different colors; larger battery capacity; multi-mic array providing for advanced wind noise reduction and clearer communications while on 
2 calls, messaging, or voice control; and an assignable action button that enables users to more easily invoke Artificial Intelligence (“AI”). Further, the glasses are quite similar to those covered by HQ H355075, dated March 31, 2026. The only differences are the number of components on the main logic board (“MLB”); the overall number of components; the country in which final assembly, final software uploads testing, and packout (“FATP”) occurs; and the percentage of the overall components cost attributable to each country from which components are sourced. The Smart Glasses will be equipped with a [ ] chipset that is mounted to the main logic board (“MLB”) within the product through System in Package (“SiP”) manufacturing in either Vietnam or Taiwan. SiP is a printed circuit board (“PCB”) substrate with mounted and enclosed electronic elements, whereas a traditional printed circuit board assembly’s (“PCBA’s”) components are mounted to the PCB but not enclosed. SiP manufacturing is an advanced technology in which surface mount technology (“SMT”) remains a critical step. SiP manufacturing does not replace SMT but rather builds on the traditional SMT process and allows for certain enhancements by using a different substrate and incorporating an advanced encapsulation and electromagnetic interference (“EMI”) shielding process. The SiP process allows the importer to reduce the space between components by almost a half, making the MLB more compact. The chipset is the primary processing and communication hub for the Smart Glasses and is responsible for the flow of data, power, and communication between the other major components of the MLB. In addition to the chipset, the MLB will incorporate a WiFi SiP which is a single, compact electronic component that integrates multiples chips along with passive components and an antenna, all within a single package to perform the complete function of a Wi-Fi system; a microcontroller unit (“MCU”) that handles lower computing functionalities such as sensor processing, audio playback, and microphone capture; and an embedded Multi-Media Card (“eMMC”) memory chip that provides the necessary memory storage. The importer states that the MLB performs the same functions as the MLB of the smart glasses addressed in HQ H348154. However, the SiP process takes approximately 20 times longer than the SMT process at issue in HQ H348154. The Importer states that the MLB drives the functioning of the Smart Glasses through visual and audio feedback from several other components, including the: ? Flexible Printed Circuit Assemblies (“FPCAs”). The FPCAs are responsible for connecting the different components of the Smart Glasses to each other and directly or indirectly to the MLB. The left input/output FPCA contains the left side microphone, interconnects for the left speaker module for audio, and a wireless debug chipset that allows for debug at the factory and the service centers. The right input/output FPCA hosts the right side microphone, interconnects for the right speaker modules for audio, and capacitive touch sensors for user touch inputs. The SuperFlex FPCA ties the electronics on the right temple arm to the left temple arm where the MLB is located. The SuperFlex also connects the MLB to the camera module and other input/output sensors such as status indicators.

The SLB is an auxiliary PCBA in the Smart Glasses’ right temple arm that is responsible for connecting the SuperFlex FPCA to the right input/output FPCA. The SLB contains an action button that allows the user to activate video or streaming, a second action button to activate AI, and a “capture” button to capture photos and video with the camera. ? Camera. The camera is responsible for the photo and video capturing capabilities of the Smart Glasses. The main processing unit on the MLB processes data feedback from the camera sensor that is required to capture a picture. ? Microphones. The microphones are responsible for the audio and voice capturing capabilities of the Smart Glasses, as well as providing noise cancellation. The main processing unit on the MLB computes voice and audio from the microphones that starts the activation of the AI. The processing unit on the MLB processes questions and inquiries by the user. Some requests must be fulfilled by the importer’s servers (e.g., AI queries requiring more processing bandwidth). These are transmitted from the main processing unit to the user’s smartphone by wireless Bluetooth connectivity and then sent from the smartphone to back-end servers for fulfillment and a response. AI’s response is then transferred back to the phone, and then back to the main processing unit of the Smart Glasses that then plays out of the speaker. ? Speakers. The speakers are responsible for the sound output of the Smart Glasses. The chipset on the MLB is critical to managing the input data and communicating with the other components so the user can access key device functionalities, such as playing audio through the speakers. ? Batteries. The batteries are responsible for storing energy that can be used to power the Smart Glasses. The Chipset on the MLB ensures efficient energy usage as it controls the power management system of the batteries. The batteries incorporated into the Smart Glasses are steel can batteries. The manufacturing steps for the Smart Glasses will occur in four stages: (1) the production of individual components in at least eight countries; (2) production of the MLB in the SiP Country (either Taiwan or Vietnam); (3) concurrent production of the SLB in Vietnam; and (4) final assembly, final software uploads testing, and packout (“FATP”) in China or Thailand. The Smart Glasses will consist of between 600 and 650 components sourced from at least nine different countries including Taiwan, China, the United States, South Korea, Japan, Ireland, Germany, the Philippines, and Malaysia. Chinese-origin components contribute 51.12 percent of the cost, Taiwanese-origin components contribute 43.55 percent of the cost, and a majority of the components are from Japan. Components in the MLB include the chipset, PCB, memory, other electrical engineering components (“EE”), critical integrated circuits (“ICs”), on-board mechanicals, and interconnect. Components in the SLB include the PCB, interconnect, critical ICs, and other EE. Components from China include flexes, battery, camera, mechanicals (frame, hinges, logo, nose pad, buttons), audio, PCB, interconnect, critical ICs, on-board mechanicals, and other 
4 EE. Components from Taiwan include the chipset, PCB, memory, other EE, and critical ICs. While the bare PCBs account for less than two percent of the total bill of materials cost, between 400 and 500 components for the MLB and between 20 and 30 of the components for the SLB are surface mounted to the PCBs through SiP and SMT to create the finished PCBAs that ultimately drive the operation of the Smart Glasses.
