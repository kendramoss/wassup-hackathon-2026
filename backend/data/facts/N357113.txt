In your letter dated December 17, 2025, you requested a country of origin ruling on three pairs of pliers, which are identified as 5" needle nose pliers, 11" long reach long nose pliers, and 4.5" mini long nose pliers. Pictures of the tools in different stages of manufacture were included with your submission, along with a narrative of the manufacturing process. All three pliers have tapered jaws that are used to grip, bend, or twist wire or small objects. They are made of two pieces of steel, with each piece providing half of the handles and half of the pair of jaws. When joined together, the joint provides a fulcrum that transfers and increases the force applied to the handles by the user to the jaws. You have stated that the manufacturing process will be the same for the three pairs of pliers. The manufacturing process involves drop-forging raw steel rods in Thailand into the shape and form of the two halves of the pliers (i.e., the pieces incorporating the handles and jaws). They are further trimmed and punched before being sent to China, where they are drilled with holes for assembly and the teeth are milled into the jaws. Subsequently, the two halves are assembled by a rivet machine and then heat treated and polished. Finally, the handles are coated in plastic, the pliers are laser marked with a logo and then packaged for retail sale.
