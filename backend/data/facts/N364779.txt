In your letter dated September 2, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on an on automotive carrier on behalf of Seohan Innobility Co., Ltd. The article under consideration is identified as the LX3 Carrier, part number 52715-P9000, which is designed for use in the rear suspension system of the LX3 vehicle model. The carrier provides mounting points for various suspension, steering, braking, and wheel-speed detection components, including the lower arm, upper arm, assist arm, trailing arm, constant velocity joint, wheel speed sensor, and caliper. The carrier also supports the real wheel load of the vehicle. According to the information provided, the LX3 Carrier is manufactured in South Korea from an LX3 Cast Carrier, part number M52715-P9000. Production of the cast carrier begins with aluminum ingot, aluminum scrap, strontium, and magnesium of Chinese origin. These materials are imported into South Korea for further processing. In South Korea, the aluminum ingot and scrap are melted and combined with the strontium and magnesium additives. The molten aluminum undergoes modification and degassing treatments before being subjected to an electromagnetic-stirring high-pressure casting process. During the casting operation, the prepared aluminum is formed into the specifically shaped LX3 Carrier. The casting is subsequently cooled, trimmed to remove the gates and excess material, and heat treated. The completed carrier then undergoes visual and X-ray inspections before being packaged for shipment. The LX3 Cast Carrier then undergoes two dedicated machining operations. The first operation includes machining the reference face, boring the bearing bore, spot-drilling the four-hole pattern, machining the ABS-hole area, and milling and drilling the T/arm mounting area. The second operation includes additional machining of the four-hole pattern, U/arm and T/arm mounting areas, drain hole, and ABS hole. Upon completion of these machining operations, the article becomes the finished LX3 Carrier, part number 52715-P9000.
