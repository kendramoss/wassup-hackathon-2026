In your letter dated August 10, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on a constant velocity joint subassembly. The merchandise under consideration is a spherical-type constant velocity (“CV”) joint sub-assembly (Product No. 8107156), designed for incorporation into an automotive drive shaft. The subassembly consists of an outer race, inner race, cage, and balls, all constructed of carbon steel. The subject CV joint subassembly measures approximately 163.6 mm by 106 mm by 106 mm and weighs approximately 2.166 kilograms. A drive shaft transfers rotational force from the vehicle’s transmission to the drive wheels. The subject CV joint subassembly is incorporated into the drive shaft and allows rotational force to be transmitted at a constant angular velocity while accommodating changes in the angle of the drive shaft resulting from steering and suspension movement. The outer and inner races contain precision raceways, or ball tracks, along which the balls travel. The cage maintains and guides the balls at specified intervals. You state that the raceways formed in the outer and inner races are critical to the function and performance of the CV joint because they determine the rolling paths of the balls and significantly affect the transmission of rotational force. You propose two manufacturing scenarios. Scenario 1 Under scenario 1, the outer race begins as a carbon-steel round bar of Chinese origin. The round bar is exported to the Republic of Korea, where it undergoes cutting and heat treatment followed by hot forging, which forms the material into the required general shape of the outer race. Following forging, the outer race undergoes shot blasting and turning. A rolling operation forms the spline and ring groove on the shank. The outer race then undergoes ball-grove rough and finish machining, during which the precision raceways, or ball tracks, are formed through cutting operations. Additional heat treatment is performed to achieve the
required final hardness and wear resistance. Internal and external-diameter grinding establishes the final dimensional and geometric accuracy of the article, while ball-groove grinding achieves the final shape and surface accuracy of the raceways. The outer race is subsequently painted, cleaned, and inspected in Korea. The inner race is manufactured in China from Chinese-origin material. The cage and balls are manufactured in the Republic of Korea from Korean-origin material. The finished outer race, inner race, cage and balls are assembled in the Republic Korea to form the subject CV joint subassembly. The completed subassembly is inspected in Korea for rotational performance, operating angle, assembly condition, and other functional and quality requirements. Scenario 2 Under scenario 2, the manufacturing process differs with respect to the initial production of the outer race. A carbon-steel round bar of Chinese origin undergoes cutting and heating treatment in China. The material is then warm forged and cold sized in China to form the general shape of the outer race. The resulting semi-finished outer-race forging is exported to the Republic of Korea. In Korea, the semi-finished forging undergoes shot blasting and turning to further machine the article into the required product shape. Rolling operations form the spline and ring groove on the shank. Ball-groove rough and finish machining is then performed in Korea to form the precision raceways, or ball tracks, through cutting operations. The outer race subsequently undergoes heat treatment in Korea to achieve the required final hardness and wear resistance. Inter and external-diameter grinding establishes the final dimensional and geometric accuracy of the article. Ball-groove grinding is then performed to achieve the final shape and surface accuracy of the raceways. The outer race is subsequently painted, cleaned, and inspected in Korea. As in Scenario 1, the inner race is manufactured in China, while the cage and balls are manufactured in the Republic of Korea. The components are assembled in Korea to form the subject CV joint subassembly, which is then inspected for rotational performance, operating angle, assembly condition, and other functional and quality requirements. In both scenarios, the spline is formed on the outer race in Korea.
