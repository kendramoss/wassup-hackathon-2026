In your letter dated December 13, 2016, you requested a ruling on the tariff classification and NAFTA status of television-type cameras from Canada.The merchandise under consideration is the Allied Vision Prosilica GT-Series Cameras. These machine-vision cameras, capture video images in real time and transmit them via an incorporated gigabit Ethernet connection to a location outside the cameras for remote viewing and/or recording. Additionally, each model within the Prosilica GT-series only broadcasts a streaming motion image at differing frame rates and resolutions, based on the model type chosen within the series. Moreover, none of the Prosilica GT-series cameras have the capability to record any images nor have any measuring or checking functionality. The GT-series camera uses global shutter, which captures the entire frame with a CCD sensor. These cameras are intended for use in various applications, such as in speed measurement, license plate recognition, outdoor imaging, traffic imaging / Intelligent Traffic Systems (ITS), public security and surveillance, industrial inspection, machine vision, military and space applications, microscopy, and medical and healthcare. The components originate from various countries including Canada and the U.S. The individual components are ordered and shipped at different times. The manufacturing steps that occur in Canada consist of the following: component preparations; programming, front assembly; loading VSUB and sensor serial number; trigger test; camera body integration; sensor X-Y alignment test; perform burn-in cycle for the camera; final test; and bagging.
