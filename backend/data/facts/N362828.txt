In your letter dated July 2, 2026, you requested a classification, country of origin, and marking ruling on behalf of your client, International Stationery Company Limited. The products under consideration are 24 round colored pencils that each measure 7.0” x 0.3” with a 4-millimeter wax/clay core. The pencils are made of wood that is painted to match the color of the core, end cut, and sharpened. They do not include ferrules or erasers. The pencils will be packaged in a tin container with a grooved plastic insert. According to the information that you provided in your letter, raw Pulai wood from Indonesia is cut to shape and size to form slats and is then shipped in bulk to Vietnam, where they are grooved to fit the pencil cores. In Vietnam, pencil cores are produced using materials sourced from China. Wax is melted and mixed with clay, pigments, and binders, then the mixture undergoes grinding, cooling, pulverizing, aging, lead extrusion, and drying to produce fully formed pencil cores. The cores are inserted into the grooved slats with glue, clamped together, and allowed to dry. Once dry, the glued slats are cut and shaped into individual pencils, then sanded, polished, and painted to match the core color. They are sharpened for use, and a logo is stamped onto the pencil barrel. The pencils are then packaged in a tin container of Chinese origin and shrink wrapped. Per your submission, the containers will be marked “Made in Vietnam,” and you ask if the individual pencils can be exempt from country of origin marking as they will be sealed in the outer container which will be marked and seen by the ultimate purchaser. 

CLASSIFICATION You suggest classification of the colored pencils under subheading 9609.10.0000, Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS). We agree.
