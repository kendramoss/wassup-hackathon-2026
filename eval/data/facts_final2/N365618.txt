In your letter dated October 1, 2026, you requested a tariff classification and country of origin determination ruling. Product information was submitted with your request. The item under consideration is a pneumatic air hammer kit (Item number 1151200006) which will be used for removing weld slag, rust, scale, cutting bolts, chiseling metal, and breaking cement. When the user engages the trigger, compressed air enters the tool and is directed to a cylinder which houses a piston. The highly compressed air forces the piston forward within the cylinder before it is returned backward in a continuous reciprocating cycle. This rapid reciprocating motion extends to the shank of an attached chisel to deliver impacts to a workpiece. You confirmed that there is no rotary mechanism or rotary output. The hammer kit features a dark gray die-cast aluminum handle, trigger-operated valve, carbon steel piston and cylinder, and a quick-change retainer spring for easy chisel replacement. Additionally, it will be imported as a kit for retail sale with four round-shank chisels packaged together in a box. As you described, the air hammer is assembled in Malaysia from Malaysian and Chinese components. In Malaysia, metal stock and rough metal blanks from China undergo CNC machining such as turning, drilling, boring, threading, and deburring to obtain the form of the finished handle body, cylinder, and piston. With regard to the handle body, aluminum ingots sourced from Malaysia are die-cast to shape prior to CNC machining. These processes deliver the precise shapes, dimensions, drilled holes, threading, slots, and surfaces which the components rely on for functioning.
Additionally, several Chinese-origin components are imported to Malaysia ready for assembly or consolidation into the kit without further processing. These components include, but are not limited to the trigger, the valve assembly, the sealing plug, the air intake nut, springs, pins, O-rings, and the chisel attachments. Assembly begins with the installation of the piston and valve assembly into the cylinder round. After that, the cylinder round is installed onto the handle body. The sealing plug is installed onto the valve stem, and a spring is installed onto the sealing plug. At that point, the valve stem, the air intake nut, the control valve, and the trigger are sequentially installed into the handle body and secured with pins. Assembly ends with the installation of a chisel attachment and the retainer spring onto the cylinder round. Finally, the completed pneumatic air hammer is tested for function and accuracy and packaged with the four chisels in a box for export to the United States.
