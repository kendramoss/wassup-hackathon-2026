In your letter dated September 18, 2026, you requested a tariff classification, country-of-origin, and marking determination ruling. The items under consideration have been identified as various bias ply paddle tires used for sand racing, and sold under the name “Skat Trak”. You indicate that these tires are sourced from China, Thailand, Taiwan, and the United States, and shipped to Canada. The tires from China, Thailand, and Taiwan are new, treaded tires, while the tires sourced from the United States are used racing slicks. After importation into the United States from Canada, all of these tires will be used on All-Terrain Vehicles (ATVs), Utility Terrain Vehicles (UTVs), motor vehicles, and motorcycles. The tires are designed and marketed for “off-road” use and are not designed to be used on public roads and ways. After shipment from their source countries to Canada, the original tread tires and outer rubber on the new tires are mechanically ground and buffed away. The surface of the used racing slicks is also mechanically ground and buffed to remove any residual racing compound, which is used for racing on asphalt. The casing on both, the new and used tires, is ground down to the tire’s cords. The grinding operation creates numerous small surface lines and irregularities that provide mechanical texture and improve adhesion. After grinding, the following steps are taken: 1. The prepared casing is measured and marked to establish the precise location and alignment of each paddle. 2. Adhesive is applied to the prepared bonding area to provide the initial bond between the casing and the new paddle rubber. 3. The rubber, which is sourced in Canada, is measured and cut in-house for each individual paddle. 4. The prepared rubber is positioned on the casing according to the alignment marks. 5.
5. The paddles are applied individually, one at a time. 6. The casing and paddle rubber are then placed into a specialized forming die and vulcanizing press. 7. Heat and pressure are applied to form the rubber into the paddle profile and permanently vulcanize it onto the prepared casing. 8. Specialized dies determine the paddle profile while the press applies heat and pressure. 9. Forming pressure is approximately 10–50 tons depending on paddle size and design. 10. Operating temperature is approximately 300–325°F. Each paddle requires approximately 11–30 minutes of vulcanizing/cure time. 11. After all paddles have been formed and vulcanized, the completed tire is inspected to verify proper bonding, paddle placement, and straightness. The process is repeated for each paddle until the required configuration is completed. You state that finished tires may contain 8–22 paddles. Finished tire sizes range from approximately 18 inches to 36 inches. Your company currently produces 17 individual paddle styles for different applications. The end result of this process in Canada is the formation and manufacturing of paddles which are permanently affixed and vulcanized onto the surface of the imported tires.
