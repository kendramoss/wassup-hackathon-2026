In your letter dated October 21, 2026, you requested a tariff classification ruling. Photographs were submitted along with your request. The merchandise includes: the Man Rock Mickey Coffee Cups, material number 10-4719-8403, the Ice Cup, material number 10-4721-0651, and the Easter Ed Cup, material number 14-8627-4869. All of the cups are made of porcelain ceramic. Item #1, the Man Rock Mickey Coffee Cups, material number 10-4719-8403, is imported as a two-piece set. The white porcelain cup features an image of Minnie Mouse, and the black porcelain cup features an image of Mickey Mouse. Each cup measures approximately 3.58 inches high by 3.22 inches in diameter at its lip, tapering to a base 2.16 inches in diameter. Neither cup has a handle; each holds 360 milliliters, and the set of two is valued at $12.78. Item 2, the Ice Cup, material 10-4721-9651, is a white porcelain cup with a handle. It measures approximately 3.70 inches high by 3.62 inches in diameter at its lip, tapering to a base 2.40 inches in diameter. The cup has a capacity of 380 milliliters and is valued at $5.60. Item 3, the Easter Ed, material number 14-8627-4869, is a white porcelain cup with a handle. It measures approximately 3.74 inches high by 3.93 inches in diameter at its lip, tapering to a base 1.97 inches in diameter. The cup has a capacity of 490 milliliters and is valued at $6.40. The drinking vessel features an image of a rabbit and an Easter motif. The cup’s country of origin is Vietnam. In your ruling request, you refer to these drinking vessels as cups. However, the tariff term “mug” is defined as a straight-sided or barrel-shaped vessel measuring about the same across the top as the bottom, usually heavier than a cup, with a heavier handle, having a flat bottom and not used with a saucer (Ross Products,
Inc. v. United States, 40 Cust. Ct. 158, C.D. 1976 (April 3, 1958)). Based on its dimensions, all of the cups do not meet the definition of a mug.
