In your letter dated October 30, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling. The merchandise under consideration is identified as the “BitchBoy,” which is described as a Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) controller for music and visual jockeys (VJ). The subject device is comprised of a control printed circuit board assembly (PCBA) within an enclosure. On the outside of the enclosure, the device incorporates 49 key switches, 12 slide potentiometers, 8 rotary potentiometers, a capacitive trackpad, LEDs, and a haptic vibration motor. In use, the subject controller is attached to a computer via USB connection and acts as a music and visual signal interface for VJs. The input music or visual signal is altered via the multiple encoders and potentiometers on the device. This is performed in conjunction with the software installed on the computer. In your request, you state that the manufacturing process begins in China with the creation of the PCBA via surface mount technology (SMT) and reflow-soldering. This process places the electrical and electronic components onto the board, including the microcontroller module, multiplexers, LEDs, diodes, among others. The PCBA is then sent to the Netherlands where the slide and rotary potentiometers are soldered onto the board along with the key switches. The enclosure is then created via 3D printing and assembled around the board prior to the mounting of the trackpad and haptic motor. The finished device is then programed, tested, and packaged for shipment to the U.S.
