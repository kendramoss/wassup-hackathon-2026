In your letter dated September 20, 2026, on behalf of Spectrum Brands, Inc., you requested a country of origin ruling on electric garment irons for purposes of current trade remedies. The product involved here is the Black & Decker Easy Steam Nonstick Iron (the “iron”). This garment steam iron is used to heat and flatten garments, thereby eliminating any wrinkles or creases in the fabric. The iron is assembled in Vietnam using components manufactured in China and Vietnam. The Chinese-origin components include the soleplate, PCB (Printed Circuit Board), thermostat, inner wires, bimetal chip, and connectors. The balance of components are produced in Vietnam, which include the whole product assembly, water tank assembly (including the water tank), lower handle, water tank bottom, top and front covers, skirt, and lamp cover. The soleplate is manufactured in China, where its substrate is die-cast, polished, coated, and assembled with a quartz tube that heats the finished soleplate. Next, the soleplate, along with other Chinese-origin components, is shipped to Vietnam. In Vietnam, various plastic parts are injection-molded. A steam cover is then attached to the soleplate with a pressing and forming machine, and room-temperature vulcanizing glue is applied. The soleplate is then welded and screwed to the various other components, including the injection-molded plastic parts, a printed circuit board, a water tank assembly, and a power cord, to create the final iron. You state that the manufacturing process in Vietnam involves operations to snap the parts together, secure them with screws, and package the finished product for retail sale.
