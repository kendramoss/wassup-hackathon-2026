In your letter dated October 1, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling for the purposes of the applicability of trade remedies on behalf of your client, Confluent Medical Technologies (“CMT”). The products to be imported are nickel-titanium (NiTi or nitinol) alloy tubes and L605 (cobalt chrome) tubes used for medical devices and surgical implants. Based on the information provided, the nitinol tubes and cobalt chrome tubes are manufactured in a similar fashion. Regarding the nitinol alloy tubes, you state that nickel and titanium are melted and formed into bars by a manufacturer in the United States (U.S.). The bars are then gun drilled into tube hollows by CMT and then exported to Germany or Costa Rica where they are cold-drawn to elongate the tubes, which reduces the diameter and wall thickness. Next, the tubes may be ground to remove the oxide layer (degreased) and then undergo full annealing and final annealing to define the mechanical properties and straighten them. Finally, the tubes are inspected, packaged, and then imported into the U.S. for further processing by CMT. The tubes have a diameter of 0.50 inch (12.7 mm) and will be imported in lengths ranging for 42 – 48 inches. Concerning the L605 (cobalt chrome) tubes, you indicate that cobalt, chrome, and other elements are melted and formed into bars by a manufacturer in the U.S. The bars are then gun drilled into tube hollows by CMT and then exported to Germany or Costa Rica where they are cold-drawn, degreased, full annealed, straightened, inspected, packaged and ultimately returned to the U.S.
