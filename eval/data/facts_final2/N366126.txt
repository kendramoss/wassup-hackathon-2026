In your letter, dated October 16, 2026, you requested a country of origin of a textile grocery bag holder on behalf of your client, HeaNeam DX Co., Ltd. In lieu of a sample, photographs of the product were provided to this office. The item, described as a “Fabric plastic bag holder,” is a textile bag holder designed for the storage and the dispensing of plastic grocery bags. The elongated tube-like bag is composed of 100 percent cotton Oxford woven printed fabric. The bag measures 8 ½ inches wide and 20 inches long, excluding the webbing loop hanger, and features an elasticized opening at both ends. At the top, the bag features an elasticized opening with a plastic cord lock, and a 100 percent cotton webbing loop to facilitate hanging. At the bottom of the bag, the elasticized cord is double knotted. You state the grocery bag holder is available in three different printed fabric designs and colors: model number ST-PBH-HY (flower design) is available in blue, sky blue, and pink; model number ST-PBH-GC (gingham check design) is available in green, beige, and gray; and model number ST-PBH-SR (stripe design) is available in green, beige, and gray. You state the manufacturing operations for the textile grocery bag holder are as follows: Brazil: 100 percent cotton yarns are spun and shipped in spools to South Korea. China: Plastic-lock is produced and shipped to South Korea. South Korea: 100 percent cotton Oxford fabric is woven, scoured, bleached, printed and washed. The fabric is cut, sewn, and assembled into a tube-like bag. The 100 percent cotton webbing fabric is woven, cut into hanging loops and stitched to the bag.
The 100 percent cotton sewing thread is produced. The 70 percent spandex and 30 percent polyester elastic fabric is produced, cut, and sewn on both ends of the bag. The plastic cord lock is attached to the elastic cord at the top of the bag. The 100 percent polyester brand label is woven, cut, and sewn to the bag. The finished bag is inspected, packaged, and exported to the United States.
