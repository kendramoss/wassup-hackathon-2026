In your letter dated September 4, 2026, you requested a country of origin ruling on a complete strut assembly on behalf of your client Jinbo Auto Parts (Thailand) Co., Ltd. The article under consideration is a complete strut assembly (CSA), Part Number S0143-A0011, designed for use on Ford-150 (2004-2008) and Lincoln Mark LT (2006-2008). The CSA is a primary automotive suspension component, combining a coil spring and a shock absorber (strut) into a single integrated unit. It is installed on the front axle of vehicles with independent suspension setups. Its function is to bear vehicle weight, absorb vertical road impacts and vibrations, maintain tire contact with the road surface, and provide vehicle stability and passenger comfort. The CSA consists of a coil spring, strut, mounts, bumpers, and dust cover. The manufacturing processes and final assembly occurs in Thailand. In Thailand, the raw material and purchased components from China are received and inspected. The bottom cover is press-fitted onto the outer/reservoir tube using a high-precision press machine, followed by press forming to the required dimensions, and welded. The welded assemblies undergo 100% leak testing to detect microscopic weld failures. Following the assembly, the inner walls of the tubes undergo multi-stage ultrasonic cleaning to remove all particles and contaminants. The completed outer reservoir tube is assembled with related housing components to form the reservoir tube sub-assembly. The base valve assembly is a sub-assembly comprising multiple discs, washers, valves, seals, and rings assembled and riveted together in a precise sequence. The piston rod, manufactured from raw round steel through CNC machining, tapping, drilling, boring, and grinding, is assembled with multiple components to create a functional piston rod assembly: A rebound limit ring is precision-welded to the piston rod. The welded rod undergoes multi-stage ultrasonic cleaning to remove weld residue and particles that could damage seals. The loading guider sub-assembly is pressed onto the piston rod. The oil seal is lubricated and installed onto the rod.
The rod valve pack is assembled onto the rod following the same critical sequencing as the base valve assembly. A specialty locking nut is torqued to a precise specification; incorrect torque results in poor damping performance and testing failure. Nuts are punch-deformed to permanently secure the valve pack and prevent slippage. The piston rod is transformed from a machined steel rod into a functional suspension sub-assembly through welding, cleaning, assembly, torquing, and locking. The reservoir tube sub-assembly is filled with precisely specified absorber oil using an automated oil injection machine, and the piston rod assembly is inserted. Oil specification is critical to damping performance: different viscosity grades affect compression and rebound response, temperature tolerance, and seal longevity. After oil filling, the guide is pre-tightened to set the correct compression force on the oil seal. The assembled unit then undergoes 100% damping force testing to verify performance against specifications. The outer tube is then sealed by flanging, permanently enclosing the internal assembly. Finally, nitrogen is charged into the sealed unit. Nitrogen charging prevents hydraulic fluid foaming, resulting in more consistent and durable suspension control. The result is a completed bare shock absorber. The completed bare shock absorber is loaded onto an automated painting line, spray-painted for corrosion protection and finishing. Following that, the dust cover and dust shield are installed to protect the piston rod. Then, the anti-collision pad is installed to buffer at maximum compression, followed by the ring and ring bushing. Finally, the coil spring and upper/lower spring bumpers are installed using a coil spring press machine, and the strut mount and spring disc are installed and secured with top nuts torqued to specification. The finished CSA is labeled and packaged per customer specifications, and the finished goods are then transferred to inventory for shipment.
