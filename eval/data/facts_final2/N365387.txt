In your letter dated September 24, 2026, on behalf of your client, Nita, you requested a tariff classification and country of origin determination ruling. The item under consideration is described as the XP Series inline labeling system, specifically the XP-200T (item #3568), which is designed to automatically apply pressure-sensitive labels to bottles and other containers moving through a production or packaging process. While there are several different variations within the XP Series, they are all manufactured and function in a substantially similar manner with the only difference being the size and quantity of labeling heads. In the representative configuration there will be two NITA AE-916 MK2 pressure-sensitive wipe-on labeling heads; a powered conveyor and associated product guide rails; Pathfinder product-handling assemblies; a Top Clamping Belt (TCB) and related adjustment assemblies; product sensors and label-sensing equipment; side applicator and foam-roller assemblies; an electrical enclosure and machine-control system; a panel PC HMI running NITA productivity software; and the machine frame, head-adjustment systems, safety controls and associated structural components. NITA commercial literature describes the XP inline series as a fully integrated system combining conveyance, product handling, NITA labeling heads and the NITA productivity software/HMI. The pressure-sensitive wipe-on labeling head (item AE-916 MK2), is the core of the system and is designed to dispense and apply pressure-sensitive labels to bottles and other containers. The device manages the label web from the initial supply roll, through the application process, and finally to the recovery of the spent backing liner. During operation, the labeling head feeds and advances the label web under controlled tension, utilizing a sensor to detect the position of individual labels and index the web to the appropriate application position. At the peel plate, the backing liner changes direction, causing the pressure-sensitive label to separate from the liner and be presented for application, where it is then applied to the container using a wipe-on process. The labeling head incorporates principal mechanical and electromechanical systems,
including an unwind system, web-tensioning and guiding mechanisms, a servo-driven web-advance system, and a spent-liner rewind system, which may be operated via an individual external control interface or connected to a common control system serving multiple heads. The unit is designed to operate in conjunction with a conveyor or other container-handling system that moves the product through the labeling position to complete the application. The conveyor, product-handling systems and controls support, stabilize and coordinate the labeling operation but do not themselves dispense, peel, present, or apply the labels. The systems are constructed from materials sourced from Canada, the United States, Japan, China, Hungary, and South Korea. The Canadian components include minor assembly components and the plastic support for the clear-label sensor. The US components include the labeling heads, clear label sensor kit and connector, plastic labels, routing clamps, washers, leveling pads, tubes, nuts, screws, caps, machine key, couplings, belt, servo, metering belt motor, springs, dowels, brackets, and rubber sheet. The web break sensors are of Japanese origin while the product presence sensor is from Hungary. Chinese components include the labeling junction box, tower light, buzzer, filter assembly and fan, split collar, split miter gear, collar assembly, handle assemblies, collar assemblies, rods, plates, flanges, shafts, knobs, clamps, cable grips, buttons, electric harness, plastic enclosure, caps, pulley assemblies, spacers, timing belt, and roller foam. Finally, the components sourced from South Korea include the dispenser motors, servo, and belt motor. The comprehensive manufacturing process occurs in Canada and begins with the fabrication of machine frames and associated structural elements, the in-house machining of selected components to engineering drawings, and the preparation, fitting, inspection, and adjustment of structural and mounting components. Following this initial phase, workers proceed with assembly and system integration into the final configuration, which involves installing and aligning powered conveyors, guide rails, and product-handling systems; positioning U.S.-manufactured AE-916 MK2 labeling heads on their adjustment assemblies; and integrating side applicators, sensors, safety devices, electrical enclosures, HMIs, and machine controls through complete electrical interconnection and mechanical alignment. Finally, the fully assembled machine undergoes calibration and functional testing at the Canadian facility to verify the coordinated operation of all systems under the intended machine configuration, ensuring that any necessary mechanical, control, or operating adjustments are completed prior to final approval and release for shipment. CLASSIFICATION Section XVI, Note 4, Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS), addresses functional units. The note states “Where a machine...consists of individual components (whether separate or interconnected by piping, by transmission devices, by electric cables or by other devices) intended to contribute together to a clearly defined function covered by one of the headings in chapter 84 or chapter 85, then the whole falls to be classified in the heading appropriate to that function”. The Explanatory Notes state that a “functional unit” covers only those “machines and combination of machines essential to the performance of the function specific to the functional unit as a whole...”
