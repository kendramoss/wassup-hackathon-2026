In your letter dated October 1, 2026, you requested a tariff classification and country of origin determination ruling. Informational material was submitted with your request. The products under consideration are stencil plates identified as the “Metal Guitar Chord Stencil.” The plates are made of 0.3 millimeter (mm) thick stainless steel, rectangular in shape, and measures 84 mm in length and 55 mm in width. Each stencil includes three sets of vertical straight slits that are meant to resemble the fretboards of three different stringed instruments: guitar, banjo, and ukulele. The slits allow users to draw straight lines for each designated instrument so they can transcribe string chords for composing music. In the final product, an image of each instrument is printed next to the set of slits it represents. There are 6 slits on the upper left half of the plate representing the guitar, 5 slits in the middle on the lower half representing the banjo, and 4 slits on the upper right half representing the ukulele. The stencil is available in three colors, blue, red, or clear. The manufacturing process for these stencil plates begins in China. In this country the overall shape of the plates is cut from stainless steel sheets. Once the plates are cut, they undergo further processing where the corners are rounded, and slits representing the fretboards are cut into the plate. The plates are then shipped to the United Kingdom (UK) where the images of the instruments are printed onto them, undergo final inspection, and finally packed and shipped to the United States. In your submission, you suggest the stencil plates should be classified under heading 7326, Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS), which provides for “Other articles of iron or steel.” We agree.
