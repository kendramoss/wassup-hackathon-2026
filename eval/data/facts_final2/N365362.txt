In your letter dated September 24, 2026, on behalf of Sebela Women’s Health, Inc., you requested a country of origin ruling and marking of an intrauterine system (IUS). Additional information was also provided by email. The product under consideration is Miudella, an intrauterine system (IUS) that is a T-shaped, implantable, and removable contraceptive device. It consists of a flexible nickel-titanium (nitinol) frame, copper sleeves that provide contraceptive efficacy, a retrieval thread, and a preloaded SLIMSERT inserter with a 3.7 mm tube. The flexible, T-shaped frame measures approximately 32 mm wide by 30 mm high and is designed to flatten within the inserter to facilitate passage through the cervix before returning to its intended shape after placement. The T-shape positions the copper sleeves within the uterus to maximize their contraceptive effect. Copper ions released from the sleeves interfere with sperm transport and fertilization, helping to prevent pregnancy for up to three years. Manufacturing begins with copper rods of Peruvian origin that are shipped to Costa Rica, where they are machined into three types of copper sleeves: short straight sleeves, short chamfered sleeves, and long straight sleeves. The sleeves are imported into Puerto Rico as hollow copper cylinders. In Puerto Rico, the Costa Rican copper sleeves are mechanically crimped onto the U.S. origin nitinol frame. The sleeves are automatically indexed in the correct sequence and loaded onto each side of the untwisted side of the nitinol frame, manually positioned, and secured. Excess frame material is trimmed, and the frame is retwisted. A U.S. origin retrieval thread is attached to the bottom of the frame, and the assembly is cleaned. The SLIMSERT inserter which contains the outer sheath, inner shaft, metal inset and pad printing, is also originated and extruded in the United States, and assembled at the Puerto Rico facility. The IUS is loaded into an extruded, shape-set, pad-printed outer sheath. The injection-molded components and an extruded inner shaft are then assembled to create the inserter. The IUS is placed within the inserter and then ultrasonically welded between two injection-molded handles, completing the integrated IUS-in-inserter unit.
The completed unit is sealed in a thermoformed tray, packaged in an individual shelf box and shipper boxes, and palletized. It is then transported to a separate Puerto Rico facility for ethylene oxide sterilization. Following sterilization, the finished product is shipped to a different U.S. facility for distribution. As you noted in your email, the U.S. origin costs account for 83.6% of the total cost of the materials.
