In your letter dated September 30, 2026, you requested a tariff classification and country of origin ruling determination for a men’s dress shoe. Photographs, a bill of materials, a description of the manufacturing process and a completed sample pair were submitted with your letter. Style No. DILL18401 5609 – TRVTL, identified as the ‘“Travis” Penny Loafer,’ is a men’s closed-toe, closed-heel, slip-on dress shoe that does not cover the ankle. The shoe is composed of a 100 percent bovine leather upper and a rubber outer sole. The upper features a saddle strap across the vamp with a slit. The Travis Penny Loafer is manufactured using unformed uppers and rubber outsoles of Brazilian origin, which are shipped to Paraguay where the uppers are lasted (shaped), and permanently attached to the outsoles to create completed footwear. The value is said to be $36.07 per pair. You state in your letter that the shoe is of welt construction, featuring a welt strip running around the outside edge where the upper and outsole meet, and that the outsole is cemented to the welt. However, you confirmed that the welt strip is not sewn to a lip on the surface of the insole and is instead applied manually via cement by an operator during assembly in Paraguay. U.S. Additional Note 1(a) to Chapter 64 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS) defines the term “welt footwear” as footwear constructed with a welt which extends around the edge of the tread portion of the sole, in which both the welt and the shoe upper are sewn to a lip on the surface of the insole, and the outsole of which is sewed or cemented to the welt.
