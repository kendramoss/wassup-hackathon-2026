In your letter, dated October 14, 2026, you requested a binding classification, country of origin, and application of trade programs ruling on wood engineered flooring cores. Information and photos were submitted for our review. The product under consideration is two-layer panels comprising the core of engineered flooring. The panels consist of red pine, white pine, basswood, or SPF blocks adhered to a veneer backing of eucalyptus wood. The blocks and veneer are the backing to which a hardwood veneer will be affixed in the United States to form finished flooring panels. While the panel does consist of a veneer and lumber blocks, it does not meet the definition of a veneered panel, as set forth in the Explanatory Notes to the Harmonized System for heading 4412: a veneered panel is a panel “consisting of a thin veneer of wood affixed to a base, usually of inferior wood, by glueing under pressure. Wood veneered on to a base other than wood (e.g., panels of plastics) is also classified here provided it is the veneer which gives the panel its essential character.” The veneer is not affixed to a base; the veneer is the base. Also, the veneer does not impart the essential character of the panel.
