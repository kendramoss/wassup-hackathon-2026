In your letter dated September 30, 2026, you requested a tariff classification ruling for the CSC drop gel block. In lieu of the sample, photographs, exploded view diagrams and manual were provided with your ruling request. Item #024999, identified as the “CSC Drop Gel Block,” is an interchangeable sealing component designed for use in Clearfield brand CraftSmart® Splice Closure dome closures. It provides tool-free cable sealing by utilizing a quick, heat-free gel compression seal around fiber drop cables. Additionally, it offers environmental protection to safeguard internal splices, slack storage, and trays from moisture, dust, and harsh outdoor elements in both above-ground (aerial, pedestal) and below-ground (handhole, manhole, or direct-bury) applications. The drop gel block is made of an injection-molded plastic body with a polydimethylsiloxane (“PDMS”) silicone gel core. It is offered in a standard red gel block and an optional blue drop gel block. The standard red gel block features 8 drops per block, supports cable outer diameters or widths between 0.12 and 0.90 inches, and has a glass-transition temperature of approximately 129.3 degrees Celsius. The blue gel block features 16 flat drops or 24 round drops per block, supports cable outer diameters or widths between 0.12 and 0.50 inches, and has a glass-transition temperature of approximately 120.1 degrees Celsius. You suggested the classification “CSC Drop Gel Block,” is classified under subheading 4017.00.0000, Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (“HTSUS”), which provides for “Hard rubber (for example, ebonite) in all forms, including waste and scrap; articles of hard rubber.” We disagree. The product is noted to be composed of silicone. Silicone is not a rubber product. In your letter you describe silicone as a rubber material. Note 4 to Chapter 40, HTSUS, describes synthetic rubber as applying to unsaturated synthetic substances which can be irreversibly transformed by vulcanization with sulfur into non-thermoplastic substances which, at a temperature between 18 and 29 degrees Centigrade, will not break on being extended to three times their original length and will return, after being extended to twice their original length, within a
period of 5 minutes, to a length not greater than 1-1/2 times their original length. Silicone is not unsaturated and thus is not cross-linkable with sulfur.
